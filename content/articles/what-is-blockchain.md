## Think of a shared notebook

Imagine a chama (savings group) where every member keeps an identical notebook of every contribution and payout. Whenever a new transaction happens, everyone checks that the sender actually has the balance and writes the new line in their book.

If one person tries to erase a page in their notebook at home, everyone else's notebook will show the truth. A **blockchain** works on this same idea, using computers and mathematics instead of paper notebooks.

## Why is it called a "chain of blocks"?

1. **Transactions happen:** Someone sends digital assets from one wallet address to another.
2. **A block is formed:** Valid transactions from around the world are grouped together into a "block".
3. **A digital fingerprint links them:** Each new block includes a cryptographic fingerprint (called a *hash*) of the previous block.

Because every block points to the block before it, altering an old record would break every block that came after it.

## Public vs. private records

On a public blockchain like Bitcoin or Ethereum, anyone can look up a transaction hash to verify when it happened and which wallet addresses were involved. That makes it transparent, while also meaning you should be thoughtful about privacy and security.
