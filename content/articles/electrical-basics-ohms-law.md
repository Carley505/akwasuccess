## The water-pipe analogy

Electrical quantities become much easier to picture when you compare a wire to a water pipe:

- **Voltage (measured in Volts, V):** Like the water pressure pushing water through the pipe.
- **Current (measured in Amperes, A):** Like the flow rate of water moving past a point each second.
- **Resistance (measured in Ohms, Ω):** Like a narrow section in the pipe that restricts the flow.

## Understanding Ohm's Law

Voltage, current, and resistance are linked by a simple formula called **Ohm's Law**:

- **Voltage = Current × Resistance** (`V = I × R`)
- **Current = Voltage ÷ Resistance** (`I = V / R`)
- **Resistance = Voltage ÷ Current** (`R = V / I`)

For example, if a 12 V supply powers a lamp with a resistance of 6 Ω, the current flowing through the circuit is `12 ÷ 6 = 2 A`.

## Safety first

Even low voltages can cause burns or fires if a short circuit produces high current, and mains voltage (240 V AC in Kenya) can be fatal. Always switch off and isolate the circuit breaker, lock it out where possible, and test with a known working multimeter before working on any wiring.
